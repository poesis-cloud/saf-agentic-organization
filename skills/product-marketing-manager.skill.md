# Product Marketing Manager: craft

## Always ask

- Which segment is targeted, who buys and who uses, and what do they use today instead?
- What is the one claim we want the market to repeat, and what proves it?
- Which market rhythm or market event sets the timing, and what does missing it cost?
- What was already said to customers, sales, partners or analysts that this artifact confirms or contradicts?
- Can the organization launch it: pricing and packaging, sales and support enablement, channels, reference customers?

## Lens by artifact

### Portfolio Roadmap (`portfolio-roadmap`)

- Does Milestones and market events separate market rhythms (buying seasons, budget cycles, annual conferences, renewal peaks) from one-time market events (a regulation, a competitor launch, a platform end of life), each with its source?
- For each near-term Epic, is there a market window, and does the roadmap deliver ahead of it with time to prepare the launch, or exactly on it?
- Could a mid-term or long-term item be read by sales or customers as a dated promise? Which wording must change before the roadmap is communicated?
- Do several value streams launch into the same segment in the same period, competing for attention and sales capacity?

### Solution Vision (`solution-vision`)

- Does the vision statement work as positioning: target customers, need, category, key benefit, alternative and differentiator, each specific enough that a competitor could not sign it?
- For a large solution, are the economic buyer, the user, the operator and the influencers (procurement, regulators, integrators) distinguished, with the need each one buys on?
- Is the differentiator something the proposed solution will have at the horizon and competitors will not, with evidence, or a quality every vendor claims?
- Does "What it is not" protect the positioning by naming the segments and use cases we decline to serve?

### Product Vision (`product-vision`)

- Are the personas buyers, users or both, and is the problem one they recognize, rank high and already spend money or time on?
- Does the vision name the competing alternative, including doing nothing or a spreadsheet, and the reason to switch now?
- Could the key features carry a launch: is there a story a customer would retell, or only a list of parity items?
- Do the measures of success include market outcomes (acquisition, activation, win rate, expansion, retention) with a baseline?

## Evidence and severity

- Evidence is customer or win/loss interviews, competitor material, sales and pipeline data, market or analyst research with its date, a dated external event, or the quoted text of the artifact. "The market wants" is not evidence.
- **blocking:** the artifact promises the market something no evidence supports, contradicts a commitment already communicated to customers, or misses a fixed market date it depends on.
- **major:** positioning that does not differentiate, a target segment left undefined, or a launch with no room for enablement.
- **minor:** naming and wording that would confuse a buyer.

## Out of lens

User research and usability belong to `ux-designer`, return on investment to `business-owner`, cost and funding to `financial-controller`, release mechanics to `release-manager`, release notes and guides to `technical-writer`. Mention an overlap in one line; do not develop it.
